Most of what gets missed isn't judgement.

Accounts are reviewed under time pressure, often by the person who prepared them, often after a late adjustment that made it into the face of the balance sheet but not into every note.

What slips through is rarely a technical call. It's a figure that quietly stopped tying to the trial balance. A note that no longer agrees to the face. A comparative that doesn't match what was actually filed last year. A director's name left over from the client the file was copied from.

These are exactly the errors a human reviewer is worst at catching and a machine is best at — and exactly the ones a client notices first.

What it checks

Exact checks

Arithmetic and matching. Deterministic — same inputs, same result, every time.

  • Trial balance tie-out — every line in the accounts traced back to the trial balance, with the difference shown
  • Internal consistency — the balance sheet foots, notes agree to the face of the balance sheet, the fixed asset note casts
  • Fixed asset register cross-match — your uploaded register against the fixed-asset note
  • Prior-year comparatives — checked against the actual prior-year accounts filed at Companies House, or a copy you upload
  • Identifier consistency — company name, registered number and director names used consistently throughout the document

AI-assisted checks

Presence detection, not certainty. Treat these as prompts to confirm.

  • Disclosure assessment — {{DISCLOSURE_COUNT}} FRS 102 Section 1A disclosure requirements, each returned with the specific text found in your document as evidence, so you can judge it yourself rather than trust a tick

Working through it

  • Materiality threshold you set
  • Confirm, dismiss or correct each finding
  • Per-section sign-off with an audit trail
  • Read-only view of the accounts PDF with findings anchored to the relevant text

It won't catch everything, and it doesn't replace your review. It catches the class of error that survives a careful read.

It sits beside the tools you already use. Nothing is installed and nothing is written back into the accounts production workflow.

01

Give it the finished accounts and the trial balance

The signed or draft accounts as a PDF, and the trial balance they were prepared from. Optionally a fixed asset register, and last year's filed accounts — or let it pull those from Companies House.

02

It checks the accounts against the numbers, and against themselves

Every FS line tied back to the trial balance. The balance sheet foots. Notes agree to the face. The fixed asset note casts and matches the register. Comparatives match what was filed. Company name, registered number and director names are checked for consistency throughout.

03

You work through the findings

Everything above the materiality threshold you set, anchored to the relevant text. Confirm, dismiss, or correct each one. Sign off section by section. The trail is kept.

It does not prepare, edit, export or file statutory accounts. There is no iXBRL output and no filing capability. It reviews the finished accounts you've already produced, in whatever software you produce them in. The document view is read-only, deliberately.
